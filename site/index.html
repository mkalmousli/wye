The whole trick

Two tiny boards and three wires.

One tiny Raspberry Pi Pico plugs into each computer. Wye sets them up for you: no soldering, no terminal, no drivers.

Nothing to install over there

The other computer just sees a keyboard.

So it works where software can't: locked-down work laptops, offline machines, and before the OS even starts: BIOS, boot menus, disk passwords.

Your whole desk comes along

Hear it. Talk to it. Share files.

Its sound plays on your speakers, your mic joins its calls, and a shared folder syncs both ways. One set of everything.

FAQ

Good questions.

What do I need?

Two Raspberry Pi Pico boards, three jumper wires and two USB cables. The app installs everything onto the boards and shows you which wires go where.

Do I install anything on the other computer?

No. It sees a standard USB keyboard, mouse, sound card and drive. Windows, macOS, Linux, ChromeOS, tablets, even BIOS screens.

Will I notice lag?

No. Wye adds about 2 milliseconds; a single frame on a 60 Hz screen takes 16.

Does it need Wi-Fi or a network?

No. Everything crosses the wire between the two boards. The app only goes online to check for updates, and you can turn that off.

Is it open source?

Yes. Wye is free software under the GNU GPL, version 3 or later. You can use it for anything, with no license key or account.

Can I build it myself?

Yes. Clone the repository and run ./build.c. Everything, firmware included, is built from the source in one repository.
